## How it works for a uniform maker

You **make school uniforms** and sell them to schools (on credit, from your school price list) and to parents (at the till, by M-Pesa or **lipa polepole**). You buy **materials in bulk** (fabric, buttons, thread, zips, labels), give them to your **tailors**, and pay the tailors for each piece they finish. Zaroda follows the materials out of your store and the finished uniforms back in, works out what each piece really cost, and keeps track of what you owe every tailor.

### Set up (once)
1. **Products, your materials**: fabric sold by the **metre** (choose "sold by weight/volume" so it takes decimals), buttons and labels by the piece, thread by the cone. Buy them through **Purchase Orders** so their cost is always the price you paid.
2. **Products, your uniforms**: one product per garment (Shirt, Shorts, Skirt, Sweater, Tunic...) with an **option for each size** (size 10, 12, 14...). Put them on your **School price list** as you would any item.
3. **What one piece uses**: open each size's **Recipe** (Products → Recipe), choose **Made in advance and kept in stock**, list the materials ONE piece uses (e.g. *1.2 metres of grey fabric, 7 buttons, 1 label*) and set the **tailor's pay per piece**.
4. **Sets**: make a product such as *Form 1 Boys Full Set* with a size option, open its Recipe, keep **Made when sold**, and list the pieces in the set (e.g. *2 x Shirt size 12, 1 x Shorts size 12, 1 x Sweater size 12*). Give the set its own price. Selling or delivering one set takes those pieces out of stock; you never stock the set itself.
5. **Tailoring → Tailors**: add each person who sews for you, with their phone number.

### Making uniforms, step by step
1. **Give a job** (Tailoring → Jobs → *Give a tailor a job*): the tailor, the piece and size, how many, and the pay per piece. Zaroda shows the materials the job needs against what is in the store, and refuses a job you do not have the materials for. When you give it out, **the materials leave your stock**.
2. **Take in finished pieces**: as the tailor brings pieces back (all at once or in batches), enter the **good** pieces and any **rejected** ones. Good pieces go **into stock**, and the tailor earns their pay for each good piece. Rejected pieces earn nothing and do not go into stock.
3. **What a piece costs**: each good piece is costed at its share of the materials **plus** the tailor's pay, so your **Profit & loss** and the school invoices show what a uniform truly cost you.
4. **When a job will not finish**: **Close** it with the reason (the pieces still out are written off with it). A job nothing was handed in on can be **Cancelled**, and its materials go back into stock.

### Paying tailors
- **Tailoring → Tailors** shows, for each tailor, the pieces made, the pieces still in their hands, and **what you owe them**. **Statement** lists every hand-in and payment with the running balance.
- **Tailoring → Pay**: record each payment (M-Pesa, cash or bank, with the M-Pesa code). Paying more than is owed counts as an **advance** against their next pieces. A wrong payment is **voided** (with the reason), never edited.
- In **Accounts**, finished work adds the tailors' pay to your stock's value and to **Owed to tailors**; paying them clears it. The cost reaches your profit when the uniform is sold.

### Selling uniforms
- **To schools**: schools order from your price list (pieces or sets) and you confirm, deliver and invoice as described in the school supplier part of this guide below. When you confirm an order, the stock column for a set shows how many complete sets your pieces make.
- **To parents**: sell pieces or sets at the till. **Lipa polepole** lets a parent pay in parts and collect when it is fully paid.

### Common questions for uniform makers
**"Not enough materials" when giving a job.** The store holds less than the job needs. Receive more fabric through Purchase Orders, or give a smaller job.

**A piece has no list of materials.** Open it under Products → Recipe and choose *Made in advance and kept in stock*, then add what one piece uses.

**A set cannot be delivered.** One of its pieces is short. Give the tailors a job for that piece, or deliver what you have and the rest later (orders can be delivered in parts).
